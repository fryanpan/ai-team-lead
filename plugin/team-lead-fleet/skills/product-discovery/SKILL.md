# Product Discovery

**No solution, plan or design is complete unless it answers the product discovery questions.** A request that names its solution is still a product improvement. "Add a dark mode toggle" tells you what someone asked for, not who needs it or why.

## Questions

These questions are designed to help you do the most useful thing as fast as possible.

1. **User:** who is the user? What do they know? What do they care about? What are they good or bad at?
2. **Workflow and pain:** what is their current workflow, and where does it hurt? How often, and how badly?
3. **Today:** what do they do today instead?
4. **Success:** what does success look like, and how is it measured?
5. **Feedback loop:** how fast can you get feedback and iterate? Can you do it in hours or days?
6. **Alternatives:** what else could solve it, including doing nothing? Consider technical solutions, process changes and team changes, and check what best practices and existing solutions already exist.

## How to run it

- **For each question, summarize what the doc under discussion already says**, in four sentences or fewer. If the doc doesn't answer it, say so.
- **Ask targeted follow-ups only where an answer is missing or incomplete.** Ask about the gap, in one batch. Don't re-ask a question the doc already answers clearly.
- **Offer research only when the person can't answer a follow-up or struggles to.** Do it only if they accept, and only for that question.
- **After the answers are in, write a short case against the proposal.** Use what you learned, such as low demand, no link to current goals, or a cheaper alternative.
- **Offer suggestions.** Ask the person whether they want suggestions for measurement methods and for solutions. If so, brainstorm and research and provide them.
