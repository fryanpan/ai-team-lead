# Thin local data does not set an effect size

Bryan, 2026-11-09: *"Don't use SF's data where we haven't gotten enough data to narrow the uncertainty band. Use higher quality longer term data from elsewhere."*

A city's 16 months of speed camera data counted 53 people killed or seriously hurt before the cameras and 48 after. The 95% interval ran up to 48% more injuries. A comparison table took that upper end as its low estimate and printed "speed cameras: 32 more serious injuries a year". That is noise from a small count, and it reads as a finding.

## The rule

- **Do not size an effect from a local series whose interval spans zero or covers a wide range.** A short window or a small count usually causes this.
- **Take the effect from the strongest long-term evidence instead:** meta-analyses, and multi-year evaluations that control for regression to the mean.
- **Report the local result beside it as the local reading, with its interval**, and say whether it is consistent with the external figure.

## How to apply

- Before a figure uses a local effect, check its interval and its count.
- Never use one end of an interval as a point estimate.
